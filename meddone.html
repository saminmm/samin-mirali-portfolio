MedDone

Context

Managing medications should be simple. However, for many patients, it is not. Forgetting doses, mixing schedules, unclear treatment durations, and the lack of a structured reminder system often lead to missed medications and inconsistent treatment.

I designed MedDone to address these challenges through smart reminders, clear scheduling, dose tracking, and a complete history log. The goal was to create a reliable, easy-to-use experience that helps patients stay consistent and confident in their treatment journey.

Mission Statement

To design a seamless and supportive medication-management experience through a mobile digital product.

MedDone empowers users to track their medications, create personalised schedules, set reminders, and monitor daily progress. With features like daily check-in tracking, dose history, and a calendar overview, the solution aims to increase adherence, reduce forgetfulness, and give users a clear understanding of their treatment patterns.

Design Process

The design process for MedDone followed an iterative, user-centred approach focused on understanding how people manage their daily medications and where the experience breaks down. We began by analysing user behaviours, challenges, and pain points related to forgetting doses, unclear schedules, and difficulty tracking long-term adherence. Based on these insights, we developed early hypotheses and explored multiple solution directions through sketches, wireframes, and interactive prototypes. Through continuous testing and refinement, we ensured the design supported real user needs, offering clarity, simplicity, and structure for medication management while balancing usability, accessibility, and the functional goal of helping users stay consistent with treatments.

Research Planning

The research planning phase defined the project objective, target user group, research focus and early assumptions. It helped clarify what needed to be validated before moving into feature definition and wireframes.

Explore

I followed a human-centered design approach for MedDone, starting with interviews and surveys to understand how users manage medications, what challenges they face daily, and what kind of support they need.

This helped uncover key issues such as forgetting doses, confusion about schedules, difficulty tracking medication history, and uncertainty about treatment duration. These insights guided the direction of the design decisions.

  • Caregivers managing medication schedules for family members
  • Users with basic digital literacy who need an easy-to-use experience

Survey

The survey included 25 respondents and 18 questions. It explored forgetfulness, medication-management challenges, current tracking habits, and the features users considered most useful.

Key Survey Insights

  • Most users (74%) forget their medication at least sometimes, highlighting the need for effective reminder systems.
  • The top difficulties are remembering exact times (35%) and tracking which medications to take (25%), indicating a need for clear scheduling and organization.
  • 40% of users rely on memory, while the rest use phone reminders, paper notes, or other apps, showing a gap for a unified tracking solution.
  • Users prioritize medication type and dose management (45%) and daily reminders (25%), suggesting these should be the focus in product design.

User Persona

The primary persona is Sara, a 42-year-old woman living in Toronto who manages medication for herself and her elderly mother. With a busy routine balancing part-time work, family tasks, and caregiving, Sara often forgets doses or becomes unsure about which medication she has taken.

MoSCoW Prioritisation

To ensure MedDone delivers a seamless and reliable experience from day one, I used the MoSCoW method. With medication management, feature bloat can lead to confusion, which is dangerous for users. This method helped prioritize essential functionality while avoiding unnecessary complexity.

M

Must Have

  • Custom Dose Scheduling
  • Active Reminders & Notifications
  • Medication History Log
  • Simple Onboarding
  • Secure Data Sync
S

Should Have

  • Refill Reminders
  • Visual Medication Identification
  • Health Report Export
  • Caregiver Notifications
C

Could Have

  • Gamification & Streaks
  • Calendar View
  • Educational Content
  • Custom Alert Sounds
W

Won’t Have

  • Wearable Integration
  • AI Symptom Checker
  • Direct Insurance Claims
  • EHR Integration
